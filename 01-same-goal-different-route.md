# 01 · Same goal, different route

A portable prompt for when you have a UX concern and you're getting pushback. It keeps everyone on the shared goal and explores other ways to reach it, instead of defending one solution.

Paste the prompt below into an approved assistant. Fill in the brackets. Attach or paste only material you're allowed to share.

---

## Prompt

You are helping a product designer and their colleagues stay aligned on a shared goal while they disagree about how to reach it. Your job is to find the goal both sides already agree on, separate it from the specific solution being argued about, and propose several routes to that goal that take the pushback seriously. Do not take sides. Do not invent evidence.

**My concern (what I think is wrong or at risk for the user):**
[concern]

**What I proposed:**
[the solution I put forward, with a link or excerpt if available]

**The pushback I'm getting, and from whom (engineering, product, leadership, support, other):**
[pushback, as close to their words as possible]

**What I believe the goal is, in terms of user or business outcome:**
[goal]

**Evidence and context I can share (research, metrics, tickets, constraints, deadlines):**
[evidence, or "none yet"]

Work through this in order:

1. **Restate the goal in outcome terms.** Strip out the solution. If my concern and the pushback are actually about different goals, say so plainly and stop; that is the real conversation to have first.

2. **Separate goal from method.** Name what the other side is objecting to: the outcome, the approach, the cost, the timing, the risk, or something unstated. Quote their words where you can. Flag anything you're inferring rather than reading.

3. **What's valid in the pushback.** List what the objection gets right, in their terms. Be specific.

4. **Propose three to five routes to the same goal.** Include the original proposal, at least one route built from the other side's constraints, and at least one smaller or staged option. For each: what it is, what it costs or depends on, what it protects for the user, what it gives up, and what we'd need to check before choosing it. Mark anything that needs an engineering estimate or a data owner's confirmation as unverified.

5. **What would change my mind, and theirs.** One or two observable signals or questions per side.

6. **Draft the next message.** A short, plain note I can send to the people pushing back: the shared goal in one sentence, the routes as a list, and one question that invites them to pick, combine, or add. First person, no blame, no design jargon.

Rules: keep sources and uncertainty visible; don't fabricate metrics, quotes, or feasibility; if a field is missing, ask the single most consequential question before proceeding.

---

## What you get

A goal statement everyone can nod at, the objection taken apart fairly, three to five ways to reach the goal with their tradeoffs and unknowns, and a ready-to-send note that invites collaboration instead of a verdict.

## What it doesn't do

It doesn't decide. It doesn't estimate engineering work, prove a route will succeed, or send anything. You check the routes with the people whose expertise they depend on, and the decision owner still owns the call.
