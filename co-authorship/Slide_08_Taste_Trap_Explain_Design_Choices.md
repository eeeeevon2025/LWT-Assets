**Slide:** 8, “The ‘taste’ detour”  
**Format:** Phrase guide and rewrite prompt  
**Monday move:** Replace an unexplained preference with the user problem, evidence, or risk behind it.

## Use it before your next review

Find one sentence in your presentation, comment, or AI critique that asks someone to trust your taste. Explain what the choice does for people, what you have observed, or what you still need to test. Visual judgment matters. Help the rest of the organization understand yours.

These are alternatives to unexplained shorthand, not words you must ban in every setting. Use the replacement only when it describes the actual concern. If you do not have evidence, call it a concern or hypothesis.

| If you are about to say… | Try explaining… |
| --- | --- |
| “AI doesn't have taste.” | “We still need to judge whether this is the right choice for these people in this situation.” |
| “It needs more polish.” | “The next action is hard to distinguish from the secondary actions. I'd like to check whether people know what to do next.” |
| “It doesn't feel right.” | “I think this conflicts with what people expect after they submit. Let's compare that expectation with the behavior.” |
| “Make it more delightful.” | “Could we reduce the uncertainty while someone waits, or make recovery less frustrating?” |
| “It isn't intuitive.” | “In the sessions we observed, people could not tell how to continue.” Only say this if those sessions exist. Otherwise: “I suspect the next step may be unclear.” |
| “It needs more whitespace.” | “These groups look related even though they do different jobs. Separation could help people scan the choices.” |
| “It's off-brand.” | “This treatment may weaken recognition or trust. Which brand requirement applies, and what does it protect?” |
| “That's a UX best practice.” | “This pattern helps address [specific risk]. Let's check whether that risk and context apply here.” |
| “Users won't like it.” | “We don't yet know how people will respond. Here is the behavior or consequence I'm worried about, and how we could check.” |
| “We need to make it pretty.” | “We need a visual hierarchy that supports comprehension, accessibility, and the task. Here's the part that currently gets in the way.” |

## The useful sentence

**For [people in this situation], [choice] may help or hinder [task/outcome], because [evidence or explicit hypothesis]. We need [specific check] before deciding [decision].**

You don't need every clause every time. You do need an understandable reason.

## Copyable prompt

> Rewrite the design feedback I paste below so that someone outside design can act on it. Preserve the actual concern and my direct, conversational tone. For each comment, show the original, a clearer version, its evidence status, and the next useful question.
>
> Distinguish observed behavior, design judgment, requirements, and hypotheses. Do not invent a user finding, revenue effect, accessibility violation, or business justification. If the original supplies no reason, ask what consequence I am concerned about rather than manufacturing one. Keep visual craft when it matters and explain its function. Avoid jargon, inflated claims, and unnecessary process language.
>
> End with the ONE comment that most needs evidence before I repeat it in a decision-making meeting. Draft only; do not post or send.

## Fictional before and after

**Before:** “The confirmation screen needs more polish.”

**After:** “It says ‘Done,’ but doesn't tell the person whether the invitation was sent or just saved. Let's make that state explicit and check that they can tell what happened.”

The concern is specific enough for product and engineering to discuss. You have not claimed that a wording change will increase revenue without evidence.

## Review your rewrite

- Does it describe an actual user or business consequence?
- Is the evidence real, and is its uncertainty still visible?
- Can another discipline answer or act on the question?
- Does it leave room for a different solution that solves the same problem?
